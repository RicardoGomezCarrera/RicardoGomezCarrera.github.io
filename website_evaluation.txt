1. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
=============================================================================

The website has improved substantially since the January 2026 evaluation.
The most impactful changes are the custom domain, the addition of research
fields, the ORCID link, and the Open Graph tags. The site now compares very
favorably with top academic economics websites.

The site does several things well:
- Clean, distraction-free layout with good whitespace
- Professional headshot and clear identification
- Research fields explicitly listed on the Home page
- CV available as downloadable PDF in a dedicated directory
- Research papers hosted directly as PDFs
- Abstracts available via toggle for all papers
- Co-author links included throughout
- Custom domain (ricardogomezcarrera.com) configured
- ORCID, Google Scholar, and LinkedIn linked in the header
- Open Graph and meta description tags on all pages
- Book page with extensive media coverage is a strong differentiator
- Policy Engagement section with four high-profile institutional presentations
- Data page sharing replication materials signals openness
- Research Awards section adds credibility
- Clarifying note on Previous Working Papers resolves earlier ambiguity

2. HOME PAGE
=============================================================================

WHAT WORKS:
- Bio is concise and informative (4 short paragraphs)
- Key affiliations (PSE, World Inequality Lab) are linked
- Research fields explicitly listed: Development Economics, Labor Economics,
  Public Economics, Education Economics
- Supervisors are named and linked (Macours, Behaghel)
- WIR 2026 role is prominently mentioned with link
- Open Graph tags present for social media sharing

PROPOSALS:

(a) Consider adding a "News" or "Recent Updates" item. For example:
    "New: Presented World Inequality Report 2026 at the European Commission
    (March 2026)"
    This signals an active, maintained site. Keep it to 1-2 items max.
    This was recommended in the January evaluation and remains relevant.

(b) The About section could mention your expected graduation year if you
    are approaching the job market. Hiring committees look for this.

(c) Consider whether the World Inequality Report 2026 deserves a more
    prominent mention on the Home page, given the scale of media coverage
    and institutional presentations. A brief highlight line (e.g., "Lead
    author of the World Inequality Report 2026, covered by The Guardian,
    Bloomberg, Le Monde, and El Pais") could be very impactful for first-
    time visitors who may not navigate to the Book page.

3. RESEARCH PAGE
=============================================================================

WHAT WORKS:
- Clear section navigation at the top (Content: links)
- Logical ordering of sections (Work in Progress > Publications >
  Working Papers > Technical Notes > Previous Working Papers > Other Research)
- Abstracts toggle for every paper
- Co-author links throughout
- Media coverage links for selected papers (VoxDev, Savage Minds)
- "Other Research" label is more professional than the previous "Other
  Exercises"
- Previous Working Papers section now includes a clear explanatory note
- The anchor link in the section nav correctly uses id="other-exercises"
  for the "Other Research" heading, which works but could be updated for
  consistency

PROPOSALS:

(a) The section nav anchor text reads "Other Research" but the id attribute
    is still "other-exercises". Consider updating the id to "other-research"
    and the anchor link to "#other-research" for consistency. This is a
    minor technical cleanup.

(b) If you go on the job market, you will need to prominently feature
    your Job Market Paper (JMP) at the top of the Research page (or even
    on the Home page). The JMP should have its title, abstract, and PDF
    link immediately visible without any clicks. Plan for this.

(c) Consider adding links to SSRN, Google Scholar, or DOI pages for
    published and working papers. This helps with discoverability and
    citation tracking. The publication in the Latin American Journal of
    Central Banking already has a ScienceDirect link, which is good.

(d) The Work in Progress section currently has three items with
    descriptions. Best practice is to only list work in progress that you
    are prepared to discuss in detail during interviews. Ensure each item
    is sufficiently developed.

(e) Consider adding direct links to replication code or data where
    available (e.g., GitHub repos). Sharing replication materials is
    increasingly expected and valued.

4. BOOK PAGE
=============================================================================

WHAT WORKS:
- Strong content: lead author of a major report with media coverage from
  top international outlets (The Guardian, Bloomberg, Le Monde, El Pais,
  Al Jazeera, France 24, BBC, Channel 4, and many more)
- Policy Engagement section with four institutional presentations is
  impressive and expanded since last evaluation (now includes UN DESA,
  European Parliament, European Commission DG ECFIN, and European

- Description and My Role toggles keep the page clean
- Report cover image with link to the WIR website
- Media organized by type (Audio, Video, Print)
- Links to Full Report, Executive Summary, and Website
- "See all media coverage" link at the bottom for completeness

PROPOSALS:

(a) The France 24 video thumbnail is embedded directly from YouTube
    (img.youtube.com). Consider hosting a static thumbnail locally to
    avoid potential loading issues and to maintain control over the visual.

(b) Consider whether the full media list could be collapsed by default
    (similar to the abstract toggles) to reduce page length. The page is
    now quite long with the expanded media and policy engagement sections.

(c) The book cover image (280px) is well-sized. No change needed from
    the previous recommendation to increase size.

(d) Consider adding the date of publication (December 2025) more
    prominently near the top of the page, so visitors immediately
    understand the recency of the report.

6. EXPERIENCE PAGE
=============================================================================

WHAT WORKS:
- Education, Research Awards, and Professional Experience are well-organized
- Institution links are included
- Award entries link to the awarded papers
- Dates now included for professional roles (addresses January proposal)
- Research Awards section is a valuable addition with four awards
- Multiple roles per institution are listed

PROPOSALS:

(a) Consider adding a Teaching section if you have teaching experience

    academic job applications and its absence is noticeable on academic
    sites. This was recommended in the January evaluation and remains the
    most significant gap on this page.

(b) Consider adding skills or languages if relevant (e.g., programming
    languages: R, Stata, Python; Languages: Spanish, English, French).
    Many candidates include this.

(c) Consider adding brief descriptions of your responsibilities in key
    roles, similar to the Policy Engagement descriptions on the Book page
    (expandable descriptions). This would be especially useful for non-
    obvious roles like "Director of Domestic Debt Issuance."

(d) The Professional Experience section could benefit from a note about
    the World Inequality Lab role being concurrent with PhD studies, to
    help readers understand the timeline.

7. DESIGN AND TECHNICAL CONSIDERATIONS
=============================================================================

WHAT WORKS:
- Simple, fast-loading static HTML (no heavy frameworks)
- Consistent navigation and header across all pages
- Responsive design with media queries for mobile
- Favicon included
- Clean typography and color scheme
- Custom domain (ricardogomezcarrera.com) configured via CNAME
- Open Graph tags on all pages for social media sharing
- Meta description tags on all pages for SEO
- CV hosted in a dedicated /cv/ directory

PROPOSALS:

(a) sitemap.xml and robots.txt. The site still does not have these files.
    Adding them would help search engines index the site properly. This is
    straightforward to implement.

(b) Twitter/X or Bluesky. If you have an academic social media presence,
    consider adding it to the header links. Many economists use these for
    dissemination. Only add if you maintain a professional presence.

(c) Consider adding a "Last updated" date that is more granular (e.g.,
    "7 February 2026" is already granular, which is good). Ensure this
    date is kept current when making changes.

(d) The footer is positioned as a fixed element at the bottom of the
    left sidebar (position: fixed; bottom: 50px). On pages with long
    content (like Book and Research), this means the footer remains
    visible at all times. This is fine for desktop but verify the
    behavior on mobile devices is as intended.

(e) The Open Graph image tag on all pages points to profile.jpg. For the
    Book page specifically, consider using the WIR 2026 cover image
    instead, which would be more visually distinctive when shared on
    social media.

(f) Consider adding Twitter Card meta tags (twitter:card, twitter:title,
    twitter:description, twitter:image) alongside the Open Graph tags for
    better rendering on Twitter/X.
