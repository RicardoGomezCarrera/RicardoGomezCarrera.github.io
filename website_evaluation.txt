=============================================================================

The website is now comprehensive, professional, and compares very favorably
with top academic economics websites. All priority recommendations from the
January and March evaluations have been addressed.

The site does several things well:
- Clean, distraction-free layout with good whitespace
- Professional headshot and clear identification
- Research fields explicitly listed on the Home page
- Recent News section signals an active, maintained site
- CV available as downloadable PDF in a dedicated directory
- Research papers hosted directly as PDFs with abstracts for all papers
- Policy Writing section showcases public-facing research communication
- Co-author links included throughout
- Custom domain (ricardogomezcarrera.com) configured
- ORCID, Google Scholar, and LinkedIn linked in the header
- Open Graph, meta description, and Twitter Card tags on all pages
- sitemap.xml and robots.txt for search engine indexing
- Book page with extensive media coverage is a strong differentiator
- Policy Engagement section with four high-profile institutional
  presentations in reverse chronological order
- Media coverage list collapsible to keep the page manageable
- Awards and Fellowships section with five entries including SECIHTI
- Academic Presentations section with four entries
- Referee Service section (Journal of Development Economics)
- Clarifying note on Previous Working Papers resolves earlier ambiguity
- Data page preserved but hidden from navigation until more content exists

2. HOME PAGE
=============================================================================

WHAT WORKS:
- Bio is concise and informative (4 short paragraphs)
- Key affiliations (PSE, World Inequality Lab) are linked
- Research fields explicitly listed
- Supervisors are named and linked (Macours, Behaghel)
- WIR 2026 role is prominently mentioned with link
- Recent News section with three items (upcoming seminar, WIR print
  availability, LSE presentation) signals activity
- Open Graph and Twitter Card tags present

FUTURE CONSIDERATIONS:

(a) The About section could mention your expected graduation year if you
    are approaching the job market. Hiring committees look for this.

(b) Keep the Recent News section current: rotate items as events pass
    (move "Upcoming" items to past tense, drop oldest items). Aim for
    2-3 items maximum.

(c) When on the job market, consider a prominent WIR 2026 highlight line
    (e.g., "Lead author of the World Inequality Report 2026, covered by
    The Guardian, Bloomberg, Le Monde, and El Pais").
